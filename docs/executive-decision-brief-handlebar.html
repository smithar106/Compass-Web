How it works — and why it’s different

The failure loop we mapped

Why the old design never stays fixed

  • Cable elongation under cyclic load
  • Fixation structure loosens
  • Pad wear shifts lever free-play
  • Clamping force drifts out of spec
  • Corrupts the hall-sensor calibration signal
  • Manual re-adjustment — then repeats
The engineering fix

A closed-loop mechanical system

  • Fixed-length cabling — no factory-set drift
  • Automatic wear-compensation holds free-play
  • Consistent clamping force for full pad life
  • Stable signal — no calibration cycle
  • Zero scheduled adjustment
The deliberate choice

Reliability by subtraction

We solved this mechanically on purpose. Adding sensors or actuators would have introduced firmware, calibration drift, and new failure modes. Removing the adjustment entirely is more robust, cheaper per unit, and works identically across all three platforms with no software dependency.
